Gear & Getting Started

Gear that won't fight you

None of this is affiliate-driven. It's what actually worked, and what didn't, while learning from scratch.

Choosing a Guitar

Pick a guitar that's easy to hold and easy to tune.

A smaller-bodied acoustic or a nylon-string classical is usually gentler on beginner fingers than a full dreadnought or an electric that needs an amp to feel rewarding. Prioritize low action (string height) over looks — a guitar that's hard to press down will slow you down far more than a plain finish will.

Tuners & Accessories

A clip-on tuner earns its price back immediately.

An out-of-tune guitar makes every chord sound wrong, which makes practice discouraging for no good reason. A basic clip-on tuner, a handful of medium picks, and a soft carrying case cover almost everything a beginner actually needs.

Practice Routine

Building a routine that sticks

Consistency

Short and Often

Fifteen focused minutes a day beats one long session on the weekend — fingertips and muscle memory build gradually.

Tracking

Log What You Worked On

A quick note of which chord or song you practiced makes it easy to see progress and decide what's next.

Patience

Expect the Plateau

Progress isn't linear. A rough week doesn't mean you're not improving — it usually means the next skill is close.
